Wars, 1800-1950

The Second Taijian War

Throughout the first deacdes of the 19th century, the emperors of Taijian looked on at the rapidly growing economic and technological power of the various duchies of the Solchat with envy. The discovery that the already significant iron deposits under the control of the southern duchies were far larger than anticipated only added fuel to the fire. In line with their doctrine of seizing any large resource deposists that had driven them to invade Angal in 1795, preperations began to be made for a full scale invasion. Due to the constant rebellions coming out of the Tankan and Malsei regions, only an inexperienced force could be spared for the invasion. In an act of sheer arrogance, no extra armies were raised.

Starting in 1822, the Second Taijian War was fought between the Taijian Dynasty and the various Solchat Duchies in the Coalition of the Czar. The war opened with an invasion of the Duchy of Torianov which quickly stalled into a seige of the capital. The other assorted Duchies began mobilising immediately and sent out a relief force, split in two thanks to competeing families. One was led by the older Marshal Grigory Pampushkin, while the other was led by the new and popular Marshal Zhumets Kamarov. Pampushkin's force would quickly be relegated to the sidelines as it was too big to keep pace with Zhumets's force.

Zhumets reached Torianov just as the defending force was starting to disintegrate under the extreme pressure being put onto them by the Dynasty forces. Using a portion of his as a diverting attack, a cavalry charge of around 800 lancers slammed into the Dynasty's rear and rampaged through their camp, destroying an eye watering amount of stores and killing the enemy's leadership. The resulting panic caused the entire army to rout, scoring Zhumets a descisive victory against a force twice the size of his. Following this success, he would be elected as the Czar, the first for a hundred years and the first to be granted the title in perpituity.

The next year, the emperor of Taijian; Yu-Lao, personally led an army into Torianov, capturing it swiftly and burning the city to the ground. As this army turned to descend on the next target, Alkagrad, they recived the news that Czar Zhumets was already en route to come and fight them. Under the threat of being stuck beseiged in Torianov, Yu-Lao was forced to flee. After a suprisingly cold winter, Zhumets raced north as the first blossoms of spring began. He began marauding through the Ganhai province and would go on to decimate the local Dynastic army at the battle of Kasho Mountain. as 1824 became 1825, Zhumets marched into Yuanxi unopposed and declared that his next target was Monras.

As he marched northwards towards the provincial capital of Monsha, his scouts reported that there was a Dynastic army in the region led by the Emperor himself. After crossing the Kosel river, Zhumets found himself in a trap of sorts as the Dynastic army was sat right in his only way forwards. retreating across the river would leave him vulnerable and allow his forces to be attacked without any worry of a strong defence. Zhumets ordered his men forwards to engage the enemy. The battle was close, but as night began to fall after the first day of fighting, it seemed like Yu-Lao would win. Both men stepped out and met on the battlefield during a lull in the fighting, both men distraut by the amount of death that lay around them. Then and there, a peace was hammered out.

Zhumets was given the entire Ganhai Province as his own land, naming it the Duchy of Kamarov. All lands further along were given up and returned to Taijian. Zhumets kept the title of Czar and would go on to remain friends with Yu-Lao until his death. Yu-Lao's successor was much less amiable towards the Solchats, yet that did not bother Zhumets much. in 1860, following a brutal nine year long war, Zhumets became the sole leader of the Solchats, naming his new empire Kamarov too. it would only be eighteen more years until another war would start in Taijian.

The 1863 Eire War

In the years following the decisive victory for Angal in their war of independence, their strategists and generals began turning to Eire with hungry looks. Eire had been the single target of Angal aggresion since the 11th century, owing to a strong movement in Angal to "unify" the various Serpentis peoples for what they called the "Serpent's Empire". This started out simply as a king's excuse to go to war, but it would eventually grow into a core part of Angal racism, which went to ridiculous lengths to justify every single act of violence against anyone who wasn't an Angal. After looking around for a while, the generals collectively shrugged and decided to just go for it without even trying to make a cassus belli.
